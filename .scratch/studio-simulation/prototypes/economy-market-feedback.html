What changed and why

Explicit assumptions and deliberate omissions
  • People have finite time; equipment caps development throughput separately. Wages and facility costs continue even when people are idle. Support, platform operation, manufacturing, and a next project draw on the same time.
  • Development progress, craft, reliability, and audience fit are distinct. Extra polish needs extra work; cutting a commissioned scope requires client agreement.
  • An exhibition has visitors, public attention, and client acceptance, but no consumer unit sales. Self-funded attention makes future opportunities visible without issuing free money.
  • The commission pays 25 on signing, 15 at a development milestone, and 40 one period after acceptance. Deadline 6, reliability at least 60. Renegotiation extends the deadline by 2, reduces work by 1, and reduces final payment by 10. Cancellation returns unearned funding; this is an illustrative agreement.
  • A publisher offers 60 upfront, greater reach, and a studio royalty of 0.8 per game unit against a price of 2. The advance is recouped from that royalty before new royalty cash is due; it is not silently charged against all retail sales. Acceptance needs craft 60 and delivery by period 9. The studio retains design rights in this sample agreement; other rights terms are not simulated.
  • Finite customer pools are split between two preference groups. Availability, channel reach, audience fit, price, reliability, and competing offers affect buying; waiting remains an option. Critics affect discovery modestly and never directly set sales or audience approval.
  • The rival funds work and develops through finite people and equipment before entering the same customer pool. Its hardware needs prepaid batches with the same minimum order and lead time; its collections and care also continue after release. Rival AI and further releases are omitted. The first opening has no fictional consumer market.
  • Forecast bands are sensitivity illustrations (80–120% of current expected demand), not statistical confidence intervals. Actual sample outcomes are deterministic; the downside action halves reachable buyers. A future game should expose estimates rather than this demo's full internal figures.
  • Internal assembly costs 30 to set up and 4 per unit, uses 2 people and 2 equipment slots, and makes 2 units per period. A contractor charges 7 per unit prepaid, minimum 10, with a 2-period lead time. Goods remain owned by the studio; sales are limited by available units and cash arrives one period later.
  • Hardware sales create repair work (one job per ten units sold, rounded up per period), and installed machines accumulate further care needs at 0.04 jobs per unit per period. A job costs 1 cash and 0.5 person-time. These coarse rates are invented. Stopping production preserves support duties; abandoning support keeps them visible and harms user trust. Support lifetimes and terminal states are not selected.
  • The hardware studio delegates platform operation initially. Retaining it costs 5 per period and 1 person-time, separately from repairs. Third parties need tools, capacity, and attractive terms; investing once does not grant a free launch catalogue. One simplified developer with finite work and funds stands in for participation. Its actually released game adds 10 points of hardware usefulness to each audience; this magnitude is uncalibrated.
  • No loans, interest, tax, inflation, historical calibration, detailed skills, factory-floor simulation, general electronics, implementation architecture, or campaign scoring. This is not a balance recommendation.

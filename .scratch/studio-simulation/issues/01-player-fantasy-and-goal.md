## Question

Who is the player at the 1950 opening, what can they control as the studio grows, and what constitutes progress or success across the campaign?

## Resolution

Confirmed by the user on 2026-10-10.

- The campaign aims at building an enduring studio and a creative legacy. Financial survival supplies pressure; successful games, accumulated expertise, and influence give growth meaning. A small, respected studio can succeed without becoming the largest company.
- An open sandbox is a separate game mode in which players choose their own goals.
- The player begins in 1950 as an inventor making an experimental game demonstration, then gradually builds a studio.
- The campaign assesses the studio's legacy at its ending, recognizing beloved games, technical innovation, or sustained reputation without requiring market dominance. Financial survival keeps the studio in the campaign. The ending date and scoring details belong to later decisions.
- The player controls game concepts, project priorities, hiring, budgets, technology, and publishing choices. Employees perform the work; growth allows delegation while retaining creative and business direction.
- The player chooses a successor or keeps the current character, who may live beyond normal human lifespans. Succession is optional, not forced by historical age limits.
- Financial failure is allowed. Bank credit, commissioned work, and downsizing are meaningful recovery options.
- Permanent closure ends the run with that studio and presents its retrospective. Instead of starting a new game, the player may continue in the current decade with a new character by transferring to another company or creating a new studio.
- The world retains its history and earlier studios' achievements after closure. A new character starts with resources and reputation appropriate to the new situation, without automatically inheriting the closed studio's money, staff, or personal reputation. The final campaign retrospective covers all of the player's studio runs.
- Age is narrative flavour: keeping a character does not cause mandatory retirement, death, or declining abilities. The player can retain a character indefinitely.

## Ideas to playtest, not fixed decisions

- After closure, joining an existing company as its company leader is a candidate continuation experience. The role and its authority are not fixed yet.

## Balance concern to revisit

- Training combined with indefinite character retention could produce a character skilled enough to replace two or more workers. Keeping that character could become the default choice and make succession unattractive. This may introduce a "god" flavour; whether it is desirable and how to balance it remain open. Do not assume an age penalty, skill cap, or other remedy has been selected.

## Remaining scope

- Campaign end date, scoring details, core loop, training balance, company/studio boundaries, and continuation playtesting belong to later decisions.

## Question

Which well-supported milestones from 1950 onward most changed how computer games could be built, distributed, and sold, and what design implications should be considered before choosing the game's historical timeline model?

## Resolution

Research asset: [Historical timeline evidence](../research/historical-timeline-model.md).

The strongest anchors are 1950 exhibition computing (*Bertie the Brain*), 1962 institutional/peer-distributed games (*Spacewar!*), 1970s–80s home computers and removable-media software trade, 1990s shareware/CD-ROM/Windows graphics, and 2000s–2010s digital storefronts. The 1950 start predates both personal computers and a mass consumer game market, so its economy should be sponsorship, research access, and public exhibition. Later changes should unlock overlapping hardware families and sales channels gradually. Record dated, sourced historical events as data and let them alter costs, capabilities, reach, and competition; the degree of player-caused alternate history remains a separate decision. Source-backed detail and caveats are in the research asset.
